Your information on Courtly

Privacy notice

Updated 7 November 2026

This notice describes the information used by Courtly's account, booking and community features at courtlyph.com. Use the private account support form below for a question about your information.

Information you provide

For a player account, Courtly uses your name, player name, email, contact number, skill level, privacy preference and optional profile photo. Venue, coach and club accounts also provide business or profile details needed to operate their listings.

Bookings and registrations record the selected service, schedule, price, payment status and references. If payment proof is required, the uploaded image may contain information from your wallet or bank receipt. Upload only the relevant proof, and avoid including unrelated balances or transactions. Messages, community contributions, follows and support requests are associated with your account.

Why the service uses this information

Information is used to create and secure your account; show the listings and community features you choose to use; reserve available courts or places; allow the responsible operator to review payments; maintain booking and fee records; send account and booking notices; and respond to support requests.

The information necessary to provide a requested booking or account feature is processed to operate that service. Optional profile images, public profile choices and community contributions are controlled by what you choose to provide. Selecting a private profile does not remove the information an operator needs to manage your existing booking.

Who can see it

  • Your profile privacy setting controls player discovery. Public player cards show player-facing profile information such as nickname, skill level and profile image; they do not display your private full name, email or phone number.
  • The relevant venue or coach receives the contact, booking and payment-proof information needed to manage your reservation. Operators also handle payments and any refunds through their own payment accounts.
  • Authorised Courtly administrators can access information needed for support and platform operations. Private support requests and replies are restricted to the submitting user and these administrators.
  • Courtly uses Supabase for authentication, application records and uploaded files, Vercel for website and server hosting, and Resend for configured transactional email delivery. These services process the information needed for their roles. Infrastructure may be outside the Philippines.

Account sign-in information is stored in your browser so you can stay signed in. Sign out on shared devices. Payment proof files use controlled access rather than a public listing.

Records and retention

Profile information is kept with your account. Booking, payment, fee and support records remain available after an activity or cancellation; cancelling a booking does not erase its history. The current service does not automatically delete those records after a fixed number of days.

You can request access, correction, deletion or a restriction through private support. Requests are reviewed manually, including whether any records need to be retained for outstanding bookings, payments, disputes or applicable record-keeping requirements. A request being marked resolved is not by itself proof that underlying records were deleted; the reply should explain the action taken.

Your controls and requests

You can update available profile details and your privacy preference in your account. Use password recovery if you cannot sign in. To ask about access to your information, correction, deletion, objection or other privacy concerns, submit a private privacy or data request to Courtly. Signing in identifies the account the request relates to; do not send a password or one-time code.

For information held separately by a venue, coach or payment provider, contact that organisation using its own published details. You can also read about data-subject rights and complaints through the Philippine National Privacy Commission.
